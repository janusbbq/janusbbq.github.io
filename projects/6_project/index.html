Distribution Automation, Planning, and Reliability Evaluation

urban distribution network planning, distribution automation deployment, and evaluation of 7,000+ annual fault work orders at State Grid Fuyang (2015–2018).

State Grid Zhejiang Hangzhou Fuyang Power Supply Co. · Electronic Information Technician · Dec 2012 – Nov 2018

Over three years, Fuyang’s urban distribution network was planned for automation (2016), automated (2015–2017), and then evaluated (2018). I worked on all three stages.

The State Grid distribution automation master station: line loading, daily operating status, and a feeder single-line diagram.

Urban distribution network planning and design · Jan – Nov 2016

GIS, fault analysis

  • Assessed Fuyang’s urban distribution network with GIS and fault-analysis tools to identify where automation upgrades were needed.
  • Contributed to infrastructure and communication-pathway plans for terminal deployment and substation automation.
  • Worked with engineers and system architects to bring fault-prone-area mapping into the long-term automation strategy.

Distribution automation deployment and system integration · 2015 – 2017

Power distribution automation system

  • Handled terminal access and network configuration for distribution automation terminals and RTUs over fiber (EPON) and wireless channels, as automation terminals and communication networks were rolled out across the urban grid.
  • Supported the integration of RTUs with the centralized control system for remote fault detection and automatic restoration.
  • Analyzed the communication network structure of the switching stations and ring main units.
Remote fault detection on an overhead feeder. Pole-mounted fault indicators send current and voltage waveform recordings over GPRS to the master station software, which locates the faulted section.

Reliability performance evaluation and optimization · Jan – Apr 2018

Statistics, efficiency metrics, economic assessment

  • Analyzed more than 7,000 fault work orders a year (7,065 in 2015 vs. 7,580 in 2017) to evaluate the distribution automation system after deployment.
  • In 500-ticket samples, average fault-repair response time fell from 57 to 36.9 minutes (about 35%). Average fault-handling time fell from 88 to 67.6 minutes (about 23%).
  • Ran statistical evaluations of operational cost savings and customer-satisfaction benefits. Assessed the economic, management, and social benefits, including projected reductions in outage energy loss and line losses.
  • Contributed optimization recommendations that became a blueprint for later urban automation upgrades.

Networks and process improvement · 2013 – 2015

  • Migrated a regional network from SDH transport to dark fiber (Layer 2, STP) and upgraded access under four aggregation nodes to Layer 3. This ended recurring outages on the regional network.
  • Was a main participant in three process-improvement (QC) projects. One of them used QR-code lifecycle tracking of IT equipment to reduce asset-ledger errors.
